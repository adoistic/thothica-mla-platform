What was asked of us

The request

After our meetings in Kerala, one office asked whether we could build an online, AI-powered platform that helps the member stay connected to constituents and serve them easily, fairly and efficiently, and make them happy.

Today

What a front office actually runs on

A WhatsApp group and a register

Requests arrive by voice note, phone call, folded paper and word of mouth. They are held in a staffer's memory until they are not.

Silence as the default reply

Most people who write to a member never hear anything again. That silence, more than any failure to deliver, is what they remember.

No memory across time

What was promised at a house visit in March is gone by August. Staff change and the constituency's history leaves with them.

Money that lapses quietly

Allocations expire unspent because nobody sees the position until March.

Two jobs that never meet

Constituency casework sits in one diary. Assembly work sits in another. Neither informs the other.

Only the loud are heard

The office hears from people who know how to reach it. Whole wards go quiet and nobody notices.

The idea

Model the constituency, not the complaint

Every grievance system built so far is petition-shaped. A citizen submits, a ticket opens, a department is tagged. The office becomes a well-run post office and understands the constituency no better than before. We model the place itself, and let the queue fall out of it.

Diagram: a single request links to a household in ward 12, to a transformer asset, to the responsible authority KSEB Aluva, to the fund head required, and to thirty-nine other reports about the same asset.

Forty complaints about water from one ward stop being forty tickets. They become one fact about a distribution line, owned by a named authority, possibly already attached to a sanctioned work. The office solves one thing instead of forty.

Structure

Three kinds of work, kept separate

A

Obligations

The state owes something. A road, a transformer, a school compound wall, a water connection.

  • Civic issues, ward by ward
  • The fund router
  • Works and payments
  • Lapse alarms
B

Presence

Someone wants the member's time, attendance, signature or name. This spends the member's own capital, not the state's.

  • Referral letters, with diligence
  • Invitations and greetings
  • Appointments and inaugurations
  • Condolences on their own path
C

Outbound

The office tells people what is available to them and what has been done.

  • Scheme alerts to those eligible
  • Works updates to the ward affected
  • Monsoon and disaster warnings
  • The monthly constituency report

Rail A exists badly in several places. Rail B has never been built anywhere. It is where an office wins or loses its reputation, and it is the reason this is a new product rather than another grievance portal.

Rail A

Civic issues, joined to the money

The everyday substance of the work: roads, transformers, street lights, water connections, drainage, school and anganwadi repairs, footpaths, bus shelters. Joining these to the funds is where the real feature sits.

The fund router

A compound wall request arrives. The system knows it is too small for the Asset Development Fund, that it fits the Special Development Fund, and that the panchayat holds an unspent line under that head this year. It drafts the proposal for whichever route the office chooses. A transformer goes to KSEB and needs no fund at all.

Closing the loop

The person who reported the wall is told, months later, that their request became a sanctioned work with a number. Nobody in India does this. It costs almost nothing once the model exists, and it is the difference between a complaint and a relationship.

Lapse alarms

Allocations that will expire unspent, raised loudly in December and January rather than discovered in March. Money lapsing is a standard and entirely avoidable embarrassment.

We have built the fund and works side before, for a state government: local area development funds across 108 constituencies, with proposals, sanctions, works and payments, rebuilt on modern .NET with about 40 screens and fund-flow intelligence, held by more than 500 tests. Figures as of 10 July 2026.

Rail B

Presence, handled with care

Referral letters

A member's letter is a reputational instrument. If it reaches a body that turns out to be fraudulent, the member's name is on it permanently.

  • Three grades of letter, weakest by default
  • A diligence file before any letter
  • Serially numbered, retrievable in year five
  • Repeat and overlap detection

Invitations and greetings

A member can attend six functions a day and will be invited to many times that. The promise is a reply inside a fixed window, never an appearance.

  • Attend, send a representative, or send a message
  • Malayalam written by a person, not generated freely
  • Death and illness on a separate, faster path
  • Print-ready cards, dispatched from the office

Appointments and inaugurations

Capacity is controlled by the office. Slots are never published for people to book into.

  • Queue position shown, not just "pending"
  • Requests batched by panchayat into each trip
  • Protocol list for who must be named on the board
  • Claims against available hours, visible weekly

The failure mode here is over-promising. What we guarantee is a warm, specific answer within forty-eight hours. Silence is the injury we are removing, and that is a promise the office can actually keep.

What happens underneath

One minute, six steps

The voice note was transcribed, classified into a fixed taxonomy, resolved to a ward, matched against the asset register, checked for duplicates and routed to the correct authority, then given a tracking number.

Why the "39 others" line matters

The system told the constituent something true that no office could have told them: their complaint is part of a pattern. That one sentence changes the experience from shouting into a void to joining a case already being worked.

Bilingual, end to end

Malayalam and English on every surface. A constituent writes or speaks in either and is answered in the same. Letters, cards and reports generate in both. The Malayalam wording is written and approved by a person once and then reused, rather than translated afresh each time.

The invitation reply is the hard one

A regret that arrives within two days, names the daughter and the date, and comes with a card, is remembered warmly. A regret that arrives late, or never, is remembered differently.

Why these three screens

The visit brief is what people remember

Being recognised by name, with your own history known, is what a constituent describes to others for years. Everything else in this system is infrastructure for that ninety seconds in the car.

Grounded, or silent

Every number traces back to a source record, and the system says it cannot answer rather than guessing. The language model writes language and never arithmetic.

Lalitha's line is the point

A promise made in March, never filed, never chased, and never mentioned again by the person who needed it. Nothing in an office today catches that.

Nothing goes quiet unnoticed

Every item carries a next date and a named owner and cannot exist without both. Political offices lose things through volume rather than malice, and this removes that.

Safeguards

Built so it cannot become something else

These are architectural facts rather than assurances, which is what makes them worth stating.

Eligibility data is collected, and it is ringfenced

Schemes in India turn on category. Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe, Other Backward Classes, minority status, widows, persons with disability, girl students, ex-servicemen. An entitlement engine that cannot read these cannot find anyone the benefit they are owed, so the system does hold them. What matters is the discipline around them.

  • Asked only on the entitlement path, with consent that names the scheme. Never asked to file a complaint.
  • Held against the eligibility record and mirroring the government certificate, not attached to the person as a label.
  • Usable in one direction only. The system answers who qualifies for a given scheme. It cannot list, filter, rank, segment or export people by community or religion.
  • No party affiliation field exists anywhere in the design, and none ever will.

Every sensitive read is logged

Who opened it, when, and against which scheme. The office can produce that log on demand. If anyone ever alleges the data was used for something other than getting people their entitlements, the record answers for itself.

Whoever funds it sees nothing

In writing. The moment a constituent believes a supporter can read their complaint, the intake channel dies, and the intake channel is the whole system.

Data belongs to the office, not the person

With a defined handover on a change of member, mirroring the rule under which sanctioned works continue across a change of member.

Consent, retention and a code of conduct switch

Built to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023. Category data is retained for the life of the application and no longer. A number given to file a complaint is not a number given for broadcast. Outbound freezes automatically during election periods.

Getting started

How the first constituency comes alive

01

Two to three weeks on the ground

Someone works the constituency with panchayat and municipality records, building the asset and institution register: every school, anganwadi, health centre, road, transformer and water scheme, and every organisation with its office bearers. No MLA office in India holds this. It is the expensive part and it is what makes everything else intelligent from day one.

02

A working demonstrator first

Before any commitment, we build a running system for the constituency on synthetic data, using the same method by which we reconstructed 250,000 rows for a state portal that could share nothing. You send a voice note into it and watch what comes back.

03

Then the office runs on it

The measure of success in week one is simple: the staff stop reaching for the register. Malayalam and English are built together from the first day rather than one being added later.

V1
Bilingual intake with visible status. Ward map. Civic issues and the fund router. Invitations and appointments with the reply window. Referrals with the diligence file. The monthly report.
V2
Jurisdiction routing with drafted instruments. The commitment ledger. The Assembly link, where failed casework becomes a question with its numbers attached. Targeted outbound.
V3
The visit brief, once there is enough history for it to be worth reading. The public dashboard. The annual record.
For you · not for the member's copy

What we need from you

1. Is the reply window credible?

We are promising a specific, warm answer within forty-eight hours to every invitation, and never promising attendance. Does an office of this size actually clear that, or should the window be longer?

2. Referral letters, how far?

Three grades, defaulting to the weakest, with no letter issued without a diligence file. Too restrictive for how these offices really work, or about right?

3. Who sits at the desk?

How many staff, what are they comfortable using, and should the interface default to Malayalam or to English?

4. Which constituency first?

Rural, urban and mixed constituencies behave very differently. The first build shapes the product, so the choice matters more than it looks.

5. Anything we have got wrong?

The three rails came from the office's own description. If there is a fourth kind of work we have missed, now is when it is cheap to add.

6. Where should it open?

Lead with the constituent's phone or with the fund router? One is emotional and one is practical, and different members respond to different openings.

Two items we are handling on our side: the current fund rules need checking against live government orders before we build the router, and referral letters touching foreign funders need a legal opinion before we build that path.
